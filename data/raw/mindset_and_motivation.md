## All-or-nothing thinking
Believing there are only two outcomes, success or failure, can lead to setting too many goals at once or abandoning a goal entirely after a single mistake. This pattern is common during holidays or vacations and can make gradual progress feel discouraging. Instead, it helps to recognize and remind oneself of small successes and gradual progress rather than viewing progress in strictly black-and-white terms.

## Overgeneralizing and labeling
Overgeneralizing happens when a single event is assumed to always be true, for example, "I failed today, so I can't do this." Labeling happens when someone defines themselves based on one instance, such as "I couldn't walk today, so I'm just not in shape." Foods or activities can also be mislabeled as simply "good" or "bad," when in reality a healthy lifestyle is about balance. Noticing these patterns and asking whether there's another way to view the situation, with self-kindness, can help counter this thinking.

## Overlooking the positives
Having high expectations can lead to focusing only on what wasn't accomplished, for example, feeling discouraged after walking 1 mile instead of a planned 2 miles, rather than recognizing that getting active at all was a positive step. Deliberately acknowledging what went well, not just what could improve, helps sustain motivation.

## Emotional decision-making
Both positive and negative emotions can influence choices, for example, skipping a healthy choice after a stressful day. Practicing mindfulness, checking in with how one feels and considering how a choice will feel afterward, can support making a deliberate choice rather than an emotionally reactive one. Healthy rewards can also help reinforce good choices.

## Blaming yourself or others
Focusing on blame when plans don't go as expected is distracting and can lead to unhelpful negative thinking, making it harder to see a path forward. Recognizing that many factors are outside of personal control, and refocusing on problem-solving and next steps, is generally more productive than dwelling on blame.

## Positive self-talk
Internal self-talk has a meaningful effect on attitude and confidence. Practicing positive self-talk, such as "I can do it" or "it's OK to make a mistake," can help build confidence when facing challenges, even if it feels unfamiliar at first. Imagining how one would encourage a loved one can be a helpful way to practice speaking more kindly to oneself.
