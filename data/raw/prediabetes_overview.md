## What is prediabetes?
Prediabetes is a serious health condition where blood sugar levels are higher than normal but not high enough yet for a type 2 diabetes diagnosis. It increases the risk of developing type 2 diabetes, heart disease, and stroke. In the United States, about 115 million adults have prediabetes, more than 2 in 5, and roughly 8 in 10 people with prediabetes don't know they have it, since there are usually no clear symptoms. It's possible to have prediabetes for years without realizing it, often until serious health problems like type 2 diabetes appear.

## What causes prediabetes?
Insulin is a hormone that allows blood sugar to enter cells for use as energy. In prediabetes, cells don't respond normally to insulin, so the pancreas produces more insulin to compensate. Over time, the pancreas can't keep up, and blood sugar rises, setting the stage for prediabetes and, eventually, type 2 diabetes.

## Risk factors for prediabetes
A person may have a higher risk of developing prediabetes if they are overweight, are 45 years or older, have a parent or sibling with type 2 diabetes, are physically active fewer than 3 times a week, have had gestational diabetes, gave birth to a baby weighing more than 9 pounds, or have polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). Race and ethnicity are also a factor: African American, Hispanic/Latino American, American Indian, Alaska Native, Pacific Islander, and some Asian American people are at higher risk.

## Getting tested
A simple blood sugar test can confirm whether someone has prediabetes. People with any of the risk factors above should talk to their doctor about getting tested.

## Why prevention matters
Prediabetes puts someone at increased risk for type 2 diabetes, which is a serious, chronic condition that can lead to further complications such as heart disease, stroke, blindness, and kidney failure. Preventing or delaying type 2 diabetes also lowers the risk for these related conditions. Diabetes also doubles the risk of depression, and that risk increases as more diabetes-related health problems develop.

## Lifestyle change program
The CDC-led National Diabetes Prevention Program (National DPP) offers a lifestyle change program to help people with prediabetes make and sustain healthy changes. Participants work with a trained Lifestyle Coach on healthy eating, physical activity, and strategies for managing stress and staying motivated. The program has been shown to lower the risk of developing type 2 diabetes by 58% overall, and by 71% for participants over age 60.
